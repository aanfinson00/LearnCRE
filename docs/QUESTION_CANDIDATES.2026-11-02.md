## 1. Spec warehouse lease-up
*absorption · intermediate · acquisitions, development · industrial*

A 300,000 SF spec warehouse just delivered. Three other competing 300,000 SF blocks are also vacant in the submarket. Net absorption has averaged 250,000 SF per quarter. Assume all four spaces are equally competitive.

**Q: Roughly how long to lease up your building?**

- ✅ ~15 months. Fair share is 1/4 of 250,000 = 62,500 SF/qtr; 300,000 ÷ 62,500 = 4.8 quarters.
- ◻️ ~1.2 quarters. Assumes you capture all submarket absorption (300,000 ÷ 250,000).
- ◻️ ~3.6 quarters. Splits absorption three ways, forgetting your own building is one of four.
- ◻️ Can't be estimated without rent growth. Fair-share math gives a defensible base case.

**Takeaway:** Lease-up time = building size ÷ (absorption × your capture share). Count every competing block, including your own.

## 2. Shopping center vacancy to 95%
*absorption · beginner · assetManagement · retail*

A 90,000 SF neighborhood center is 80% leased (18,000 SF vacant). Inline absorption has been 3,000 SF per quarter. Your business plan targets 95% leased.

**Q: How long to reach 95%?**

- ✅ ~4.5 quarters. 95% leased allows 4,500 SF vacant, so absorb 13,500 SF ÷ 3,000.
- ◻️ 6 quarters. Absorbs all 18,000 SF, ignoring the 5% structural vacancy target.
- ◻️ 1.5 quarters. Divides 4,500 by 3,000, using the vacancy allowance as the amount to absorb.
- ◻️ Not estimable without tenant sales. Trailing leasing velocity is the standard first-pass input.

**Takeaway:** Subtract the target vacancy from current vacancy before dividing by the pace.

## 3. Choosing hotel comps

Subject: 150-key suburban select-service hotel (limited-service flag, near an office park and a regional hospital).

**Q: Which sale comp is most relevant for pricing per key?**

- ✅ A 140-key suburban select-service hotel of the same brand tier, near a hospital and office demand.
- ◻️ A 300-key downtown full-service hotel that traded at a record $/key. Different segment and demand base.
- ◻️ A 150-key airport hotel with 90% occupancy. Matches key count but not the demand driver.
- ◻️ A portfolio sale of 12 economy hotels. Entity-level pricing and a lower tier.

**Takeaway:** Hotel comps match on service tier, location type and demand generators before key count or $/key.

## 5. Percentage rent math
*lease-econ · intermediate · assetManagement · retail*

Inline tenant: $30/SF base rent, 5% percentage rent, natural breakpoint. Reported sales are $640/SF.

**Q: How much percentage rent is owed?**

- ✅ $2.00/SF. Breakpoint = $30 ÷ 5% = $600/SF; ($640 − $600) × 5% = $2.00.
- ◻️ $32.00/SF. 5% of all sales, ignoring that base rent is credited.
- ◻️ $0. Treats the $30 base rent as the breakpoint.
- ◻️ $20.00/SF. 5% of sales over $240, a miscalculated breakpoint.

**Takeaway:** Natural breakpoint = base rent ÷ percentage rate; only sales above it generate overage rent.

## 6. NNN vs modified gross offer
*lease-econ · intermediate · acquisitions · industrial*

Two offers on the same space. Tenant A: $8.50/SF NNN. Tenant B: $10.75/SF modified gross, landlord pays opex, currently $2.50/SF.

**Q: Which offer nets more to the landlord today?**

- ✅ Tenant A. B nets $10.75 − $2.50 = $8.25, so A is $0.25 higher, and A has no opex growth risk.
- ◻️ Tenant B. Headline rent is $2.25 higher, ignoring the expenses the landlord absorbs.
- ◻️ Equal. The structures differ but the credit is the same.
- ◻️ Tenant B, because gross leases are always stronger. There is no such rule.

**Takeaway:** Convert gross rents to a net basis before comparing, and check who carries expense growth.

## 7. Net effective rent with concessions
*lease-econ · intermediate · assetManagement, acquisitions · office*

10-year office lease: $40/SF NNN, 8 months free rent, $60/SF TI.

**Q: What is the simple (undiscounted) net effective rent?**

- ✅ ~$31.33/SF. ($400 − $26.67 free − $60 TI) ÷ 10.
- ◻️ $40.00/SF. Face rent only.
- ◻️ $37.33/SF. Deducts free rent but ignores TI.
- ◻️ $34.00/SF. Deducts TI but ignores free rent.

**Takeaway:** Net effective rent subtracts both free rent and TI from total rent across the term.

## 8. ADR vs occupancy sensitivity
*sensitivity · advanced · assetManagement, portfolioMgmt · hotel*

A 200-room hotel runs 70% occupancy at a $150 ADR (RevPAR $105). Compare a 5% ADR decline with a 5% relative occupancy decline.

**Q: Which hurts NOI more?**

- ✅ The ADR decline. RevPAR falls equally (5%), but lower occupancy also cuts variable costs per occupied room, while lower ADR drops almost fully to NOI.
- ◻️ Occupancy decline, because empty rooms are fixed-cost heavy. Variable costs actually fall with occupied rooms.
- ◻️ Identical, since RevPAR is identical. Ignores cost flow-through.
- ◻️ Neither matters; RevPAR alone drives value. RevPAR hides the margin difference.

**Takeaway:** Equal RevPAR changes can have different NOI effects. Rate declines flow through at higher margin than occupancy declines.

## 9. Exit cap sensitivity
*sensitivity · intermediate · acquisitions, portfolioMgmt · industrial*

Year-10 NOI is $5.0M. Exit cap assumed 5.5%; stress case 6.0%.

**Q: How much lower is the exit value in the stress case?**

- ✅ ~8.3% lower. $90.9M → $83.3M.
- ◻️ ~0.5%. Confuses 50 bps of cap with 0.5% of value.
- ◻️ ~9.1%. Uses 0.5 ÷ 5.5, which is the increase going the other way.
- ◻️ ~5%. Rule-of-thumb guess with no calculation.

**Takeaway:** Value change = 1 − (old cap ÷ new cap). Small cap moves produce large value moves.

## 10. Yield-on-cost under a cost overrun
*sensitivity · intermediate · development, portfolioMgmt · multifamily*

Project: $50M cost, $3.5M stabilized NOI (7.0% yield on cost). Market cap rate 5.5%. Costs overrun 15%.

**Q: What is the new yield on cost and spread to market cap?**

- ✅ ~6.1% yield on cost, ~60 bps spread (down from 150). $3.5M ÷ $57.5M.
- ◻️ ~6.0%, treating the overrun as a 15% NOI haircut (7.0 × 0.85).
- ◻️ ~6.85%, subtracting 15 bps directly.
- ◻️ 7.0% unchanged, because NOI is unaffected. Yield on cost depends on the cost denominator.

**Takeaway:** Overruns compress yield on cost via the denominator, and the development spread erodes quickly.
