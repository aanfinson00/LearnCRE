## Drafts

Each is a situational-case seed: prompt, correct answer, and the distractor logic.

### 1. Development · sensitivity — Which assumption breaks the yield-on-cost?
A $48M ground-up multifamily deal underwrites a 6.6% yield-on-cost against a 5.25% exit cap. Hard costs, rent, and lease-up pace could each move 10%. **Q: Which single 10% miss does the most damage to the development spread?**
- Best: Rent (−10% flows straight to NOI; spread falls ~65 bps, more than a 10% cost overrun's ~60 bps and with no cost-side offset from contingency).
- Distractors: hard-cost overrun (contingency absorbs part), lease-up delay (timing, not stabilized yield), exit cap (not a YOC input).

### 2. Development · lease-econ — Pre-leasing threshold before closing the construction loan
A 220,000 SF speculative industrial building has a lender requirement of 50% pre-leased. The sponsor has an LOI for 35% and a prospect for another 20% at a lower rent. **Q: How should the sponsor handle the gap?**
- Best: Negotiate the loan covenant against rent-adjusted NOI (DSCR/debt yield on signed leases), not a raw SF percentage; don't sign the weak-rent lease just to hit the number.
- Distractors: sign at any rent, close equity-only, waive the covenant verbally.

### 3. Development · deal-process — Change order vs. contingency
Mid-build, the GC proposes a $380K change order for an unforeseen subsurface condition. $600K of contingency remains with 40% of work left. **Q: Approve from contingency?**
- Best: Approve, but first confirm it is a true unforeseen condition (not owner-scope or GC-error), document it, and re-forecast remaining contingency against the remaining work.
- Distractors: reject all change orders, fund from the interest reserve, defer until completion.

### 4. Absorption — Is the pace sustainable?
A submarket absorbed 900 units last year, but 70% of that was one 400-unit employer relocation that is now complete. 1,200 units are under construction. **Q: What absorption pace should the underwriting use?**
- Best: Strip the one-time relocation; underwrite on the ~150/yr organic run-rate and test lease-up under that case.
- Distractors: use the trailing 900, average the last 3 years blindly, assume supply is irrelevant.

### 5. Absorption · retail — Backfilling a dark anchor
A grocery anchor (35% of GLA) goes dark with 3 years left on its lease; co-tenant clauses let inline tenants pay 50% rent after 12 months dark. **Q: What is the first priority?**
- Best: Fix the co-tenancy exposure first by locating a replacement anchor or negotiating a waiver. Dark-rent and the cascade of inline rent reductions outweigh the anchor's own rent loss.
- Distractors: sue for the remaining term, re-let inline space first, drop rents center-wide.

### 6. Comp-selection — Mixed-vintage comps for a repositioned asset
A 1985 office building was renovated to Class A standards. The only 2022-vintage comps trade at 5.5%; renovated 1980s comps at 6.5%. **Q: Which set anchors the going-in cap?**
- Best: The renovated-vintage set, adjusted for remaining functional obsolescence (floor plate, parking ratio); 2022 comps set a ceiling, not a base.
- Distractors: average both sets, use new construction only, use pre-renovation sales.

### 7. Lease-econ — Free rent vs. lower face rent
A prospect offers 10 years at $30/SF with 8 months free, or $27.50/SF with 3 months free. **Q: Which is better for the landlord's value?**
- Best: Compare net effective rent *and* mark-to-market value, since the building is priced on face rent: higher face with more free rent usually wins on valuation if the buyer capitalizes the stated rent.
- Distractors: lower face "because it's cash sooner," pick on NER alone, ignore credit.

### 8. Hotel — Flag change at renewal
A 180-key select-service hotel's franchise agreement expires in 18 months. The brand requires a $6.5M PIP; a competing brand offers $3M PIP but weaker reservations contribution. **Q: How to evaluate?**
- Best: Model both on 10-year NOI net of PIP and royalty/marketing fees, with a RevPAR index assumption per brand; PIP cost alone is not the decision.
- Distractors: pick lowest PIP, go independent, defer the decision to the last six months.

### 9. Industrial — Clear height vs. column spacing
A 32' clear, 50' × 52' column-spaced building competes with a 36' clear, 54' × 56' building at +$0.60/SF NNN. **Q: How should tenant demand drive the pricing difference?**
- Best: Rent premium is justified for e-commerce/3PL users who value cube and racking efficiency; for general distribution users the premium compresses. Underwrite by target tenant mix.
- Distractors: 36' always commands the premium, ignore column spacing, price on land basis only.

### 10. Sensitivity — Rate shock vs. cap shock
A stabilized deal has 5.5% floating debt hedged only with a 2-year cap; exit is year 5. **Q: Which sensitivity matters more for levered IRR?**
- Best: Year 3–5 debt cost post-cap expiry, because it compounds across the hold, while a ±25 bps exit cap move is a one-time effect at year 5.
- Distractors: exit cap dominates always, rate risk is hedged, rent growth is the only driver.
