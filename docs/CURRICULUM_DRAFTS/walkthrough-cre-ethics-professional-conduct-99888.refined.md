# CRE Ethics & Professional Conduct

**Category:** trust-relationships
**Audience:** career-switcher | analyst

## Setup narrative

You're the listing broker on a $12,000,000 office sale. The seller is a long-time client; another broker referred the deal to you. State law and your firm's code of ethics require you to disclose every dollar of compensation flowing through the transaction — co-broke splits, referral fees, and your own take. Walk the commission stack from gross to your pocket, then size the downside if any of it goes undisclosed.

## Steps

### Step 1 — Gross commission
- **Prompt:** Sale price is $12,000,000 at a 5.0% total commission. What is the gross commission generated by the deal?
- **Expected:** $600,000
- **Unit:** usd
- **Hint:** 5% of $12M. Move the decimal and halve.
- **Result description:** Gross commission is the pool every other party will split from. Anchor on this number before allocating.

### Step 2 — Listing side after co-broke
- **Prompt:** Co-broke agreement sends 50% of the gross commission to the buyer's brokerage. What is the listing side's commission?
- **Expected:** $300,000
- **Unit:** usd
- **Hint:** Half of Step 1.
- **Result description:** Co-broke splits are contractual, not discretionary. Listing brokers don't "give up" half — they offer half to procure a buyer's-side advocate.

### Step 3 — Disclosed referral fee
- **Prompt:** You must pay a 20% referral fee, in writing and disclosed to the seller, to the broker who introduced you to the client. The fee is calculated against the listing side's share. What is the referral fee?
- **Expected:** $60,000
- **Unit:** usd
- **Hint:** 20% of Step 2.
- **Result description:** Referral fees are legal and routine — but only when disclosed in advance and paid broker-to-broker. Undisclosed referrals are kickbacks.

### Step 4 — Brokerage net commission
- **Prompt:** After paying the disclosed referral, what does the listing brokerage net?
- **Expected:** $240,000
- **Unit:** usd
- **Hint:** Step 2 minus Step 3.
- **Result description:** This is the number the brokerage books as revenue on the deal — clean, disclosed, defensible.

### Step 5 — Broker's personal take-home
- **Prompt:** Your house split is 50/50 with the brokerage. What does the broker take home personally before tax?
- **Expected:** $120,000
- **Unit:** usd
- **Hint:** Half of Step 4.
- **Result description:** Personal compensation lands here. Every step above this line was a disclosure obligation — none of it was optional.

### Step 6 — Downside of one undisclosed payment
- **Prompt:** If any payment in this chain were undisclosed, the state real estate board's statutory fine is 2× the gross commission, plus disgorgement and license review. Express that fine as a multiple of the broker's take-home from Step 5.
- **Expected:** 10
- **Unit:** multiple
- **Hint:** (2 × Step 1) ÷ Step 5.
- **Result description:** One undisclosed dollar puts a 10× hit against your earnings — before license consequences. The arithmetic of integrity is asymmetric: the upside of cheating is bounded, the downside is not.

## Takeaway

Ethics in CRE isn't a vibe — it's a paper trail. Every dollar that moves between brokers, vendors, and principals has a disclosure rule attached. Run the commission stack top-to-bottom, document each split, and the math takes care of itself. Skip a disclosure to chase an extra few thousand and a single complaint zeroes out years of earnings.

## Notes for the converter

- All dollar values tie back to the $12M sale and 5% gross commission anchor.
- Step 6 is a ratio (multiple), not a dollar amount.
- Keep the disclosure framing — the ethics lesson lives in WHY each number must be visible to the client.
