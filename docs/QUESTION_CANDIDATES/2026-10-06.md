### 1. Hotel · sensitivity · intermediate · acquisitions/portfolioMgmt
**Title:** Which RevPAR driver is your downside case really about?
**Scenario:** A 180-key select-service hotel underwrites at 74% occupancy, $142 ADR. Your downside drops RevPAR 10%. Sponsor models it as ADR −10% with flat occupancy. A lender asks why not occupancy −10%.
**Q:** Why does the split between occupancy and ADR matter for NOI?
**Best:** Occupancy loss cuts revenue but saves variable costs (housekeeping, amenities, commissions); ADR loss drops almost fully to the bottom line, so ADR-driven RevPAR declines hit NOI much harder.
**Distractors:** RevPAR is RevPAR regardless of source; occupancy is always the worse driver; ADR cuts are offset by higher F&B.

### 2. Hotel · lease-econ · advanced · assetManagement
**Title:** Management vs. franchise — who owns the brand-standard capex?
**Scenario:** Hotel sale with a PIP of $6.5M due within 24 months; the buyer is comparing keeping the managed flag against converting to franchise with third-party management.
**Q:** How should the PIP and fee stack change your bid?
**Best:** Price the PIP as a day-one capital cost, then compare total fee load (base + incentive + franchise + system fees) against flag-driven RevPAR premium; don't just compare headline fee %.
**Distractors:** PIP is seller's problem; lower base fee always wins; PIP is optional.

### 3. Retail · comp-selection · intermediate · acquisitions
**Title:** Which sales comps actually price this grocery-anchored center?
**Scenario:** Subject: 92% leased, Publix anchor with 9 years remaining. Comps include a shadow-anchored center, an unanchored strip, and a grocery-anchored center 40 miles away in a growth metro.
**Q:** Which comp deserves the most weight?
**Best:** Same anchor type, term, and tenant-sales profile in a comparable trade area, even at greater distance, beats a nearby comp with different anchor credit/term.
**Distractors:** Closest by mileage; most recent trade; highest-priced to support ask.

### 4. Retail · lease-econ · advanced · assetManagement
**Title:** Co-tenancy clause: how big is the exposure if the anchor goes dark?
**Scenario:** In-line shop leases allow 50% rent reduction if the anchor closes, and termination after 12 months dark. Anchor has just filed Chapter 11.
**Q:** What's the right first step in sizing exposure?
**Best:** Map each in-line lease's co-tenancy trigger, cure period, and remedy; compute rent at risk by tenant under reduction and termination scenarios, then layer in anchor rejection probability.
**Distractors:** Assume all shops leave; ignore until rejection is final; renegotiate loan first.

### 5. Industrial · absorption · intermediate · development
**Title:** Is 4.2M SF of spec supply a problem or an opportunity?
**Scenario:** A 20M SF submarket has 8% vacancy, 2.5M SF/yr trailing net absorption, and 4.2M SF of spec under construction, 40% pre-leased.
**Q:** How do you judge delivery risk?
**Best:** Net the pre-leased space (~2.5M SF unleased spec) against annual absorption plus pipeline of build-to-suit demand; ~1 year of absorption implies manageable lease-up, with sensitivity to slower absorption.
**Distractors:** 4.2M exceeds annual absorption so overbuilt; ignore pre-leasing; vacancy is low enough that it doesn't matter.

### 6. Industrial · sensitivity · advanced · acquisitions
**Title:** Mark-to-market rents: what if the roll-down hits at the same time as the cap expands?
**Scenario:** Industrial portfolio with in-place rents 28% below market and a 3-year WALT. Base case assumes 6% annual market growth and 5.25% exit cap.
**Q:** Which sensitivity best stresses the thesis?
**Best:** Two-way table of market-rent growth vs. exit cap, since the value creation (MTM capture) and exit valuation are both driven by those two inputs and can compound.
**Distractors:** Vary only exit cap; vary only leverage; vary only opex growth.

### 7. Multifamily · absorption · beginner · development
**Title:** How many months of lease-up does the loan need?
**Scenario:** 240-unit new build, 20 units/month leasing pace, 93% stabilization target, interest reserve sized for 10 months.
**Q:** Is the interest reserve sufficient?
**Best:** 223 units ÷ 20/mo ≈ 11+ months, so the reserve is short; add contingency for concessions and seasonality.
**Distractors:** Reserve is enough since 10 months is close; absorption will accelerate; reserve irrelevant once TCO is issued.

### 8. Office · comp-selection · advanced · portfolioMgmt
**Title:** Why do lease comps and sales comps tell different stories?
**Scenario:** Class-A trophy lease comps show $78/SF NNN rents with 14 months free rent; building-sale comps show cap rates 150 bps wider than last cycle.
**Q:** How do you reconcile them?
**Best:** Convert to net effective rents (concessions, TI), and recognize sale comps reflect risk and debt costs; use both, not one, to bracket value.
**Distractors:** Use face rents; sales comps are noise; lease comps alone drive value.

### 9. Mixed · sensitivity · intermediate · mortgageUw
**Title:** Which stress breaks the loan first — NOI or rate?
**Scenario:** Floating-rate bridge loan at 1.35× DSCR with a 2.5% SOFR cap, 5.5% spread, interest-only.
**Q:** Which stress should be run first?
**Best:** Rate shock to the cap strike and spread, since the cap bounds that downside; the real vulnerability is NOI shortfall vs. the breakeven DSCR, and cap expiry/replacement cost.
**Distractors:** Rates uncapped; ignore the cap; focus on exit cap only.

### 10. Development · lease-econ · advanced · development
**Title:** Pre-leasing a build-to-suit: who carries the cost overrun?
**Scenario:** Developer signs a 15-year NNN lease on a BTS with a fixed rent set on a $38M budget; GMP contract is not yet signed and steel is trending up.
**Q:** What's the most important protection to negotiate?
**Best:** Rent escalation linked to cost outcomes (rent set as yield on cost with cap) or a GMP signed before lease finalization, so cost risk is shared or removed.
**Distractors:** Rely on contingency only; build to budget regardless of cost; delay the lease.
