### 1. Hotel: is a $10 rate cut worth the extra occupancy?
*Situational · pricing · beginner · hotel · assetManagement*

A 150-key hotel is running 68% occupancy at a $180 ADR. The GM proposes cutting rate to $170 to chase 76% occupancy, arguing the extra room-nights more than offset the discount. Variable cost per occupied room (housekeeping, amenities, OTA/distribution commission) runs $45/night.

- **Current:** $180 ADR × 68% occ
- **Proposed:** $170 ADR × 76% occ
- **Variable cost/occupied room:** $45/night

**Ask:** Does the rate cut increase or decrease GOP per available room-night, and by roughly how much?

### 2. Hotel: does the brand-mandated PIP pencil against the RevPAR lift?
*Situational · diagnostic · intermediate · hotel · development*

A brand requires a $2.4M property improvement plan (PIP) on a 180-key hotel ahead of flag renewal. Management projects the renovation lifts RevPAR Index from 95 to 115 within 18 months of completion. Declining the PIP means exiting the flag and re-flagging independently or with a lower-tier brand.

- **PIP cost:** $2.4M / 180 keys ≈ $13,300/key
- **RevPAR Index today → projected:** 95 → 115
- **Alternative:** de-flag, lose brand distribution + loyalty channel

**Ask:** What's the right framework for deciding whether to fund the PIP versus taking the non-renewal exit?

### 3. Retail: which comp actually supports your cap rate?
*Situational · comp-selection · beginner · retail · acquisitions*

Underwriting a grocery-anchored retail center. The broker OM cites three comps: (A) an unanchored strip center that traded at a 6.25% cap, (B) a grocery-anchored center 40 miles away in a materially weaker trade area at 6.0%, (C) a grocery-anchored center 3 miles away with the same anchor and similar vintage at 7.0%.

- **Comp A:** unanchored strip, 6.25% cap
- **Comp B:** grocery-anchored, weak trade area, 40 mi away, 6.0% cap
- **Comp C:** grocery-anchored, same anchor, 3 mi away, 7.0% cap

**Ask:** Which comp should anchor your cap-rate assumption, and why do the other two mislead?

### 4. Retail: how much does losing the co-tenancy trigger actually cost you?
*Situational · sensitivity · intermediate · retail · assetManagement*

A junior anchor representing 15% of a retail center's GLA is closing. Three inline tenants — 22% of remaining rent roll — hold co-tenancy clauses that let them pay 50% rent or terminate outright if anchor occupancy drops below a threshold the closure now breaches.

- **Junior anchor:** 15% of GLA, closing
- **Inline tenants w/ co-tenancy rights:** 22% of remaining rent
- **Clause remedy:** 50% rent reduction or termination right

**Ask:** How do you size the earnings-at-risk from the co-tenancy trigger, and what's your first move with the closing anchor's landlord relationship?

### 5. Industrial: is speculative development still justified at this absorption pace?
*Situational · absorption · intermediate · industrial · development*

A submarket has 12M SF of industrial inventory at 91% leased. 2.5M SF is under construction on a speculative (unleased) basis, delivering over the next 12 months. Trailing absorption has averaged 400,000 SF/quarter.

- **Existing inventory:** 12M SF @ 91% leased
- **Spec supply under construction:** 2.5M SF, delivering in 12 months
- **Trailing absorption pace:** 400,000 SF/quarter

**Ask:** At the current absorption pace, how many quarters of above-trend vacancy should you underwrite before the new supply clears — and does that change the go/no-go on breaking ground on another spec building?

### 6. Industrial: how much is an extra 4 feet of clear height actually worth?
*Situational · lease-econ · beginner · industrial · acquisitions*

Comparable bulk industrial buildings in the submarket: 32' clear height leasing at $6.50/SF NNN; 36' clear height leasing at $7.10/SF NNN. You're underwriting a 36' building.

- **32' clear height comp:** $6.50/SF NNN
- **36' clear height comp:** $7.10/SF NNN

**Ask:** How do you translate that premium into a supportable rent assumption for the subject, and what could break the comparison?

### 7. How much does a 100 bps rate spike shrink your refi proceeds?
*Situational · sensitivity · intermediate · multifamily · mortgageUw*

A multifamily asset is coming up for refinance. In-place NOI is $2.4M; the lender requires a minimum 1.25x DSCR on 30-year amortization. Today's rate is 6.0%; a 100 bps spike to 7.0% is under discussion given rate volatility between now and closing.

- **NOI:** $2.4M
- **Min DSCR:** 1.25x, 30-yr am
- **Rate scenario:** 6.0% vs. 7.0%

**Ask:** Roughly how much do maximum loan proceeds shrink from the 100 bps move, and what does that do to the sponsor's refi-and-return-equity plan?

### 8. Mixed-use: do you lock the land price before or after rezoning?
*Situational · risk · beginner · development*

A developer has land under contract for a mixed-use project, contingent on rezoning approval. The rezoning hearing is 90 days out. The seller offers to drop the contingency and close now at a $2M discount to the as-entitled price; if rezoning is later denied, the land's as-of-right value is roughly 35% below the as-entitled basis.

- **Discount to close now (waive contingency):** $2M
- **Rezoning hearing:** 90 days out
- **Value if rezoning denied:** ~35% below as-entitled basis

**Ask:** Should the developer take the $2M discount and close now, or hold the contingency through the hearing?

### 9. Office: free rent vs. a lower face rate — which actually costs you more?
*Situational · lease-econ · beginner · office · assetManagement*

Negotiating a renewal on a 20,000 SF office suite. Option A: $30/SF face rent with 6 months free, 5-year term. Option B: $27.50/SF face rent, no free rent, same term.

- **Option A:** $30/SF face, 6 mo free, 5-yr term
- **Option B:** $27.50/SF face, 0 mo free, 5-yr term

**Ask:** Which option produces the higher net effective rent, and is comparing face rates alone the right way to evaluate these two offers?

### 10. Development: which recent land trade should anchor your residual value?
*Situational · comp-selection · intermediate · multifamily · development*

Underwriting land for a to-be-built multifamily project. Two recent land comps: (A) a similarly-zoned site 1 mile away that traded 14 months ago at $45,000/unit, just before a rate run-up; (B) a site 6 miles away in a weaker submarket that traded 2 months ago at $28,000/unit.

- **Comp A:** 1 mi away, $45,000/unit, traded 14 months ago (pre rate run-up)
- **Comp B:** 6 mi away, weaker submarket, $28,000/unit, traded 2 months ago

**Ask:** Which comp should you weight more heavily in backing into residual land value, and what adjustment does the one you discount still need?
