### 1. Hotel: RevPAR up, profit down — `absorption`? no → `diagnostic` · hotel · intermediate · assetManagement
**Scenario:** A 200-key select-service hotel grew RevPAR 6% YoY (ADR +9%, occupancy −2.5 pts). GOP margin fell from 38% to 34%.
**Q:** What most likely explains revenue growth with margin compression?
- **A (best):** Rate growth was bought with higher distribution/OTA commissions and wage/service costs; fixed costs didn't flex while occupancy dropped, so incremental revenue converted at a low flow-through.
- B: ADR growth is always margin-accretive; the loss must be an accounting timing issue.
- C: Occupancy loss is irrelevant to margin because rooms are a fixed-cost business.
- D: RevPAR is a lagging metric, so the margin drop will reverse next year.
**Takeaway:** RevPAR is a revenue metric. Judge a hotel on GOP flow-through, not RevPAR alone.

### 2. Retail: sales-percentage rent breakpoint — `lease-econ` · retail · intermediate · acquisitions
**Scenario:** A tenant pays $30/SF base with 6% percentage rent over a natural breakpoint. Sales are $450/SF.
**Q:** What percentage rent is owed per SF?
- **A (best):** $0 — natural breakpoint = $30 ÷ 6% = $500/SF; sales of $450 haven't crossed it.
- B: $27 (6% × $450).
- C: $2.70 (6% × ($450 − $405)).
- D: $3 (10% of base rent).
**Takeaway:** Percentage rent only starts above breakpoint = base rent ÷ percentage rate.

### 3. Industrial: absorption vs. delivery pipeline — `absorption` · industrial · intermediate · development
**Scenario:** A 60M SF industrial market is 94% leased. 4M SF is under construction (35% preleased); trailing 4-quarter net absorption is 2.4M SF.
**Q:** What does this imply for vacancy over the next 12 months, all else equal?
- **A (best):** Vacancy likely rises — ~2.6M SF of speculative deliveries (4M × 65%) exceeds 2.4M SF of absorption, and preleased space already counts as demand that has been absorbed or committed.
- B: Vacancy falls, because 94% leased is tight.
- C: Unchanged; construction deliveries don't affect existing vacancy.
- D: Only rent growth matters, not supply.
**Takeaway:** Compare speculative deliveries to absorption, not total pipeline to absorption.

### 4. Beginner: what a cap rate tells you — `pricing` · mixed · beginner · acquisitions
**Scenario:** Two similar buildings: one sells for $10M with $600K NOI, the other for $10M with $500K NOI.
**Q:** What's the best read of the difference?
- **A (best):** The first has a 6.0% cap vs. 5.0%; the market is pricing the second as lower-risk or higher-growth, or the first as riskier.
- B: The first is overpriced.
- C: Cap rate is the IRR, so the first returns 6% annually.
- D: Nothing — same price means same value.
**Takeaway:** Cap rate = NOI ÷ price; differences reflect risk and growth expectations, not just value.

### 5. Comp selection: stale trades in a rate move — `comp-selection` · office · advanced · portfolioMgmt
**Scenario:** Your best-matching comp traded 11 months ago at a 5.5% cap. Since then, the 10-year Treasury is +90 bps, and two thinner, lower-quality trades printed at 7.0% last quarter.
**Q:** How should you use these comps?
- **A (best):** Bracket: treat 5.5% as stale-low and 7.0% as quality-penalized high; adjust the 5.5% trade for rate movement (partial pass-through) and quality, and flag the range as wide.
- B: Use the 5.5% comp only — it's the closest match.
- C: Average all three trades.
- D: Ignore comps and use the replacement-cost approach.
**Takeaway:** Time and quality adjustments beat pure similarity when the rate environment moves.

### 6. Sensitivity: which input moves IRR most — `sensitivity` · multifamily · intermediate · acquisitions
**Scenario:** A 5-year multifamily deal at a 15% IRR. Individually: ±50 bps exit cap → ±140 bps IRR; ±1% rent growth → ±60 bps IRR; ±100 bps interest rate → ±45 bps IRR.
**Q:** What should drive where you spend diligence time?
- **A (best):** Exit cap — the largest swing — then stress it with a downside case rather than only a base case.
- B: Rent growth, because it compounds.
- C: Interest rate, because debt is fixed.
- D: All equally; sensitivities are noise.
**Takeaway:** Rank sensitivities by IRR swing per plausible move, then diligence the biggest.

### 7. Development: yield-on-cost vs. market cap — `investment-thesis` · multifamily · intermediate · development
**Scenario:** A ground-up deal projects a 6.1% untrended yield-on-cost; stabilized comps trade at 5.0% caps.
**Q:** Is the development spread adequate?
- **A (best):** Yes at ~110 bps if the cost and lease-up assumptions are firm; 100–150 bps is the common minimum, but check contingency and lease-up risk.
- B: No — yield-on-cost must exceed 10%.
- C: Irrelevant; only IRR matters.
- D: Yes — any positive spread is enough.
**Takeaway:** Development spread compensates for construction and lease-up risk; typical hurdle is 100–150+ bps.

### 8. Lease-econ: net effective rent — `lease-econ` · office · intermediate · acquisitions
**Scenario:** A 10-year lease at $40/SF flat with 12 months free rent and $60/SF TI.
**Q:** Which figure is closest to the effective rent, ignoring discounting?
- **A (best):** ~$33/SF per year = ($40 × 9 yrs − $60 TI) ÷ 10.
- B: $40/SF.
- C: $36/SF (only free rent).
- D: $34/SF (only TI amortized over 10 yrs: $34).
**Takeaway:** Net effective = (total rent − concessions − TI) ÷ term; headline rent overstates economics.
> Vet: A = (360 − 60) ÷ 10 = $30/SF, not $33. Correct before porting; option D would then also need revisiting.

### 9. Beginner: DSCR basics — `risk` · mixed · beginner · mortgageUw
**Scenario:** NOI $1.2M; annual debt service $1.0M. Lender minimum DSCR is 1.25x.
**Q:** What happens?
- **A (best):** DSCR is 1.20x, below 1.25x, so the loan sizes down or fails the test.
- B: Passes; any DSCR over 1.0x is fine.
- C: DSCR is 0.83x.
- D: DSCR doesn't apply to stabilized assets.
**Takeaway:** DSCR = NOI ÷ debt service; lenders require a cushion above 1.0x.

### 10. Development: contingency use — `risk` · industrial · advanced · development
**Scenario:** At 40% complete, a 120,000 SF warehouse build has consumed 70% of its 5% hard-cost contingency, from steel pricing.
**Q:** What's the best response?
- **A (best):** Re-forecast cost-to-complete, report to lender and equity, and evaluate value-engineering or an equity top-up before the remaining risk burns the rest.
- B: Wait; contingency is for the end of the project.
- C: Draw from the interest reserve.
- D: Cut inspections to save cost.
**Takeaway:** Burn rate vs. percent complete is the early warning; escalate when contingency use runs ahead of progress.
