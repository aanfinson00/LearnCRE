## Development

### 1. Entitlement risk vs. land price (development · risk · intermediate · mixed)
**Scenario:** A site is under contract at $12M with a 180-day diligence period. Zoning allows 150 units by right; the pro forma needs 220 via a rezoning with a 9–12 month public process. The seller will not extend diligence.
**Q:** How do you structure the offer?
- ★ Convert the contract to a longer entitlement period with staged, increasingly hard deposits, or reprice the land to the by-right (150-unit) value with an earn-out for entitled density.
- Close at $12M and entitle after closing. (Puts all entitlement risk on equity.)
- Walk away; any entitlement risk is uninsurable. (Overly absolute; the risk is priceable.)
- Raise the deposit to show seriousness. (Larger deposit at risk makes the exposure worse.)
**Takeaway:** Price land on what is entitled, and pay for entitlement only as it is delivered.

### 2. Hard-cost contingency on a GMP contract (development · risk · intermediate · multifamily)
**Scenario:** GMP contract signed at 70% drawings complete; owner contingency is 3% of hard costs; lender wants 5%.
**Q:** Why is 3% likely too thin?
- ★ Incomplete drawings mean scope gaps become change orders that sit outside the GMP, so contingency must cover design-completion risk.
- GMP contracts cap all cost growth, so contingency is irrelevant. (GMP only caps the defined scope.)
- Lenders ask for 5% regardless of risk. (Not the reason.)
- Contingency should be sized to soft costs, not hard costs. (Wrong base.)

### 3. Yield-on-cost spread to market cap (development · pricing · beginner · industrial)
**Scenario:** Spec industrial build: untrended yield on cost 6.4%, market cap rate for stabilized product 5.5%.
**Q:** Is a 90 bps spread enough?
- ★ Usually thin; developers typically want 125–200 bps to compensate for lease-up, construction and entitlement risk.
- Yes, any positive spread creates value. (Ignores risk premium.)
- No, you need a spread over the debt rate only. (Wrong benchmark.)
- Spread is irrelevant if IRR exceeds 15%. (IRR can hide thin margin on cost.)

### 4. Interest reserve sizing under delay (development · sensitivity · advanced · mixed)
**Scenario:** An 18-month build carries a 7.5% floating-rate loan with an interest reserve sized to the base schedule. Permits slipped 3 months.
**Q:** What happens to the reserve?
- ★ It is exhausted early; carry grows with both time and drawn balance, so a delay costs more than 3/18ths of the reserve.
- Nothing, reserves are fixed at closing. (They run out.)
- It shrinks, since less is drawn per month. (Mis-reads the draw curve.)
- The lender funds the overrun. (Not without a modification and likely equity.)

## Retail

### 5. Percentage rent and sales volatility (asset management · diagnostic · intermediate · retail)
**Scenario:** An inline tenant pays $28/SF base plus 5% over a $400/SF natural breakpoint. Sales dropped from $440 to $395/SF.
**Q:** What does the lost percentage rent signal?
- ★ A drop below the breakpoint removes the upside but also the occupancy-cost stress test: check sales-to-rent ratio (occupancy cost) and co-tenancy exposure before renewal.
- Nothing; base rent is unchanged. (Ignores renewal risk.)
- Immediate default risk. (Premature.)
- The breakpoint should be reset. (Rewards weak performance.)

### 6. Co-tenancy clause trigger (acquisitions · risk · intermediate · retail)
**Scenario:** Several in-line tenants have co-tenancy clauses tied to the anchor staying open. The anchor lease expires in 20 months with no renewal option.
**Q:** How should the underwriting treat this?
- ★ Model a downside where the anchor goes dark: rent reductions or termination rights across the dependent tenants.
- Ignore it until the anchor gives notice. (Too late to reprice.)
- Assume renewal at market. (Aggressive without evidence.)
- Count only the anchor's rent as at risk. (Understates the knock-on effect.)

## Hotel

### 7. RevPAR growth: rate vs. occupancy (acquisitions · diagnostic · intermediate · hotel)
**Scenario:** RevPAR rose 6%; occupancy rose 5 pts (from 68% to 73%) while ADR was flat.
**Q:** Why is this lower-quality growth than a 6% ADR-led gain?
- ★ Occupancy-led growth carries higher variable costs (housekeeping, F&B, utilities), so less drops to GOP than rate-led growth.
- It's identical at the RevPAR level so no difference. (Ignores flow-through.)
- Occupancy gains are always temporary. (Not necessarily.)
- ADR gains increase management fees. (Minor effect.)

## Industrial

### 8. Rollover mark-to-market (asset management · pricing · intermediate · industrial)
**Scenario:** 40% of the rent roll expires within 24 months at $6.50/SF against market of $9.00/SF; the owner is considering a sale.
**Q:** How should a buyer treat the gap?
- ★ Credit only part of it: net out downtime, TI/LC and the chance tenants renew below market; the gap is upside, not guaranteed NOI.
- Capitalize the full gap into price. (Gives the seller all the upside.)
- Ignore it as speculative. (Leaves value on the table.)
- Use a lower cap rate to reflect it. (Mixes income and cap rate.)

## Absorption

### 9. Pre-leasing as an absorption signal (development · absorption · intermediate · industrial)
**Scenario:** A 400,000 SF spec building is 35% pre-leased 6 months from delivery; the submarket absorbed 1.2M SF last year against 1.8M SF of deliveries.
**Q:** What does this imply for lease-up?
- ★ Competing deliveries exceed demand, so the remaining 65% likely needs concessions and a longer lease-up; underwrite 12–18 months and test downside.
- 35% pre-lease means the building will stabilize on schedule. (Ignores supply.)
- The absorption figure is irrelevant to a single building. (Submarket supply competes.)
- Lower the asking rent immediately. (Premature without evidence.)

## Sensitivity

### 10. Which assumption moves IRR most? (portfolio management · sensitivity · intermediate · mixed)
**Scenario:** A 5-year value-add deal: sensitivity runs on exit cap (±50 bps), rent growth (±1%), and renovation cost (±10%). Exit cap swings IRR ±320 bps; rent growth ±180; cost ±90.
**Q:** What should the IC memo emphasize?
- ★ Exit cap is the dominant driver and largely outside the sponsor's control, so size the equity cushion and debt terms around it.
- Renovation cost, since it's controllable. (Smaller impact.)
- All three equally, to stay balanced. (Hides what matters.)
- Rent growth, because it feeds NOI. (Second-order here.)
