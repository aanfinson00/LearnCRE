## Absorption (3)

### A1. Office vacancy glide path — *intermediate · office · acquisitions*
A 6.0M SF submarket is 18% vacant. Net absorption runs ~250k SF/yr. 400k SF of new product delivers next year. **Q: About how long until vacancy reaches 12%?**
- ✅ ~2.8 yrs — base becomes 6.4M SF; leased now 4.92M; 88% target = 5.632M; 712k ÷ 250k ≈ 2.85.
- ◻️ ~1.4 yrs — ignores the 400k SF of deliveries (360k ÷ 250k).
- ◻️ ~1.6 yrs — nets deliveries against absorption but not the larger base.
- ◻️ Never — absorption is lower than supply, so vacancy can't fall.

### A2. New-building lease-up timeline — *beginner · multifamily · development*
A 240-unit building opens with zero pre-leasing. Net absorption is 14 units/month. Lender stabilization is 93% occupancy. **Q: Months to stabilize?**
- ✅ ~16 months — 0.93 × 240 = 223 units ÷ 14 ≈ 15.9.
- ◻️ ~17 months — targets 100% (240 ÷ 14).
- ◻️ ~12 months — assumes 18 units/mo from a peak-month pace.
- ◻️ ~8 months — halves the target for "average" occupancy.

### A3. Fair-share capture — *advanced · industrial · development*
Five competing spec buildings of 1.2M SF each are vacant in a market absorbing 600k SF/yr. **Q: What does fair-share capture imply for your building?**
- ✅ ~10 years — 1/5 share = 120k SF/yr; 1.2M ÷ 120k. Underwrite pricing or a differentiator to win outsized share.
- ◻️ ~2 years — assumes you capture all market absorption.
- ◻️ ~5 years — divides by five twice incorrectly.
- ◻️ Can't be estimated without rent comps.

## Comp selection (2)

### C1. Which sale anchors the value? — *intermediate · office · acquisitions*
Subject: 1985 suburban office. Comps: (A) 2019 CBD trophy at 5.0%; (B) 1988 suburban at 7.25%, closed 2 years ago; (C) 1984 suburban at 7.5%, closed 3 months ago, arm's-length; (D) 1990 suburban at 7.4%, 8 months ago, REO sale. **Q: Primary anchor?**
- ✅ C — same vintage, product and submarket, recent and arm's-length; use B and D as cross-checks.
- ◻️ A — better building, different market tier.
- ◻️ B — good product but stale in a moving rate environment.
- ◻️ D — distressed sales reflect seller motivation, not market pricing.

### C2. Which rents set market? — *beginner · retail · assetManagement*
You need market rent for a 3,000 SF inline space. Data: asking rents $38; executed new leases (similar size, last 6 months) $34 with 3 months free; renewals $31. **Q: Best basis?**
- ✅ Executed new leases, net of concessions — about $34 face, ~$32.50 effective on a 5-yr term.
- ◻️ Asking rents — aspirational, not what tenants pay.
- ◻️ Renewals — carry no TI/downtime and sit below new-deal pricing.
- ◻️ Average of all three — mixes incomparable deal types.

## Lease economics (3)

### L1. Net effective rent — *beginner · office · acquisitions*
10-yr lease at $30/SF NNN, 6 months free rent, $50/SF TI. **Q: Simple (undiscounted) NER?**
- ✅ $23.50 — (30 × 9.5 − 50) ÷ 10.
- ◻️ $28.50 — free rent only.
- ◻️ $25.00 — TI only.
- ◻️ $30.00 — headline rent.

### L2. Full-service vs NNN — *intermediate · office · assetManagement*
Offer 1: $34 full-service. Offer 2: $26 NNN, opex $9/SF flat. **Q: Which costs the tenant less?**
- ✅ Full-service — $34 vs $26 + $9 = $35, a $1/SF edge (assuming opex holds; base-year growth risk shifts the comparison).
- ◻️ NNN — lower headline rent.
- ◻️ Identical — gross-ups wash out.
- ◻️ Can't compare without the cap rate.

### L3. Rent premium to justify extra TI — *advanced · office · acquisitions*
Choosing between $20 and $60/SF TI on a 10-yr lease; landlord cost of capital is 7%. **Q: Minimum annual rent premium for the extra TI?**
- ✅ ~$5.70/SF/yr — $40 × 0.1424 (10-yr, 7% loan constant).
- ◻️ ~$4.00 — straight-line $40 ÷ 10, no cost of capital.
- ◻️ ~$2.80 — applies 7% interest only (no principal).
- ◻️ ~$8.50 — uses the full $60.

## Sensitivity (2)

### S1. Same 50 bps, different pain — *beginner · any · portfolioMgmt*
Cap rates widen 50 bps in two markets: one at 4.5%, one at 7.5%. **Q: Which sees the larger % value drop?**
- ✅ The 4.5% market — 4.5→5.0 = −10.0%, vs 7.5→8.0 = −6.25%.
- ◻️ The 7.5% market — higher cap implies higher risk.
- ◻️ Identical, because both moved 50 bps.
- ◻️ Depends on NOI growth only.

### S2. Operating leverage on revenue — *intermediate · multifamily · mortgageUw*
Revenue $3.0M, opex $1.2M (fixed in the short run), NOI $1.8M. Revenue falls 3%. **Q: Approximate value change at a constant cap?**
- ✅ About −5% — revenue −$90k flows entirely to NOI: 90 ÷ 1,800 = 5.0%.
- ◻️ −3% — assumes NOI moves with revenue.
- ◻️ −1.8% — scales by the opex ratio.
- ◻️ −7.5% — double counts the opex share.
